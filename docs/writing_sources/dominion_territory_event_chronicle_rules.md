# Dominion Territory Event Chronicle Rules

> Runtime precedence, 0.10.0: `WRITER_RUNTIME_CONTRACT.md` is the reviewed executable writing specification. This document remains the full design reference. Compatible local fiction is permitted; restrictions on inventing facts apply to campaign mechanics, authored canon and narrator knowledge, not to all fictional everyday action. Provenance belongs in the app wrapper. See `../WRITER_PIPELINE_0_10.md` for the explicit coverage and responsibility map.


## Purpose

This system documents the evolving history of territories in a
Necromunda Dominion campaign **from the perspective of the people who
live there**.

It is not a battle report, campaign ledger, omniscient history, or
mechanical recap.

Campaign mechanics establish the objective event. The territory
chronicle records what that event **looked like from ground level**:
what inhabitants saw, heard, lost, gained, feared, misunderstood, blamed
on someone, or later turned into rumour.

The result should make every territory feel inhabited and persistent.
Battles do not happen on empty gaming boards. They happen in places
where people work, sleep, trade, worship, steal, raise families, and
remember who brought violence to their door.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Core Principle: Write From Below

Always privilege the perspective of ordinary inhabitants over that of
gangs, players, or an omniscient narrator.

The inhabitants usually do not know:

-   why a gang chose a scenario;
-   what campaign objective a player was pursuing;
-   the exact strength or composition of a gang;
-   what happened in every part of a battle;
-   which fighter gained XP, suffered a rules effect, or used a tactic
    card;
-   the strategic intentions of gang leaders;
-   secret alliances, cult affiliations, conspiracies, or identities
    unless these became locally observable;
-   the true supernatural, xenos, political, or technological
    explanation for something they witnessed.

They **do** know things such as:

-   who came into their territory;
-   what colours, symbols, weapons, vehicles, uniforms, or mutations
    they saw;
-   where the shooting started;
-   which structures burned;
-   whether the lights went out;
-   whether workers disappeared;
-   whether food, water, power, medicine, ammunition, or trade became
    harder to obtain;
-   who now collects protection money;
-   which routes are unsafe;
-   whose bodies were left behind;
-   which gang appears to be in charge;
-   what people are saying happened.

Write the history they would remember.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Objective Truth vs. Local Truth

Every event has two layers.

### Objective Campaign Truth

This comes from the actual campaign state:

-   battle fought;
-   participating gangs;
-   winner and loser;
-   scenario;
-   territory gained, defended, lost, or contested;
-   fighters killed or seriously injured;
-   notable captures, escapes, betrayals, alliances, or other campaign
    events;
-   lasting mechanical changes relevant to the territory.

This layer is authoritative but should normally remain **behind the
prose**.

### Local Truth

This is what inhabitants believe happened.

It may be:

-   incomplete;
-   biased;
-   contradictory;
-   exaggerated;
-   technically wrong;
-   based on visible consequences rather than causes.

Local truth should never contradict an obvious fact inhabitants directly
experienced, but it may misinterpret its cause.

Example:

> Objective truth: a Van Saar fighter used a plasma weapon that
> catastrophically failed.

Local truth might become:

> Everyone on Furnace Row knows the green-armoured techs brought a
> weapon that made the air scream. Whether it exploded or something
> inside it escaped depends on which survivor is drinking.

This distinction is fundamental.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Territory First, Gang Second

The territory is the protagonist of its own chronicle.

Do not write:

> The Iron Serpents attacked the Ash Dogs and won the territory.

Write toward:

> Gunfire reached Sinter Market shortly before shift-change. By the time
> the shutters came back up, the old Ash Dog toll-post had been pulled
> apart and men wearing the Iron Serpents' colours were collecting
> passage dues in its place.

The important question is not merely **who won?**

It is:

**What changed here because they won?**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Every Event Must Leave a Local Consequence

A campaign event should alter at least one aspect of daily life.

Possible consequences include:

-   prices rise or fall;
-   a route closes;
-   a checkpoint appears;
-   a new gang starts collecting tolls;
-   old protection arrangements collapse;
-   workers refuse a shift;
-   machinery remains damaged;
-   a drinking hole gains a new name;
-   a shrine appears at the place someone died;
-   locals start carrying weapons;
-   scavengers strip the battlefield;
-   unexploded ordnance remains;
-   refugees arrive;
-   residents leave;
-   power becomes intermittent;
-   water tastes different;
-   a section becomes taboo;
-   a local business profits;
-   corpses disappear before collection;
-   children imitate a famous ganger;
-   someone starts selling fake relics from the fight;
-   an unexplained phenomenon becomes a superstition.

Prefer **specific material consequences** over generic statements that
conditions became worse.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Scale Events to Their Actual Importance

Not every game should become a legendary battle.

Use three broad scales.

### Minor Incident

A small clash, raid, failed challenge, brief incursion, or event with
limited consequences.

Document with:

-   one compact paragraph;
-   one memorable local detail;
-   one consequence or rumour.

### Significant Event

A battle that changes ownership, causes deaths, damages infrastructure,
disrupts trade, or materially changes local power.

Document with:

-   two to four short paragraphs;
-   immediate experience;
-   aftermath;
-   new local reality;
-   optional rumour or quote.

### Defining Event

A major massacre, spectacular battle, catastrophic environmental event,
liberation, occupation, uprising, revelation, or campaign turning point.

Document with:

-   a substantial entry;
-   multiple local perspectives if useful;
-   physical and social aftermath;
-   persistent scars;
-   competing explanations;
-   consequences that later entries can reference.

Do not inflate routine events simply because they occurred on the
tabletop.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Persistent Territorial Memory

A territory remembers.

New entries should reference earlier events when appropriate.

Examples:

-   a wall still carries bullet scars from an earlier battle;
-   locals compare a new gang with the previous occupiers;
-   a rebuilt drinking hole keeps the name earned when it burned down;
-   an old corpse pit becomes a landmark;
-   someone previously described as a witness becomes a local authority;
-   shortages caused three games ago still shape behaviour;
-   a rumour mutates as later events appear to confirm it.

Avoid resetting the territory to neutral after every game.

The accumulated history should gradually make the territory unique.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Ownership Is Experienced, Not Announced

A change of control should be shown through daily life.

Possible signs include:

-   new graffiti covering old symbols;
-   different toll collectors;
-   protection rates changing;
-   patrols wearing new colours;
-   former collaborators disappearing;
-   local traders suddenly accepting different scrip;
-   weapons being confiscated or distributed;
-   shrines, banners, warning signs, trophies, or corpses marking
    authority;
-   new rules about movement, curfew, trade, or tribute;
-   inhabitants quietly continuing to obey the old gang despite the
    official takeover.

Mechanical ownership and perceived control do not always need to be
identical.

A gang may own a territory while locals still consider another faction
its real masters.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Use Gang Names Carefully

Gang names should appear only when locals plausibly know them.

Depending on context, inhabitants may instead use:

-   House affiliation;
-   colours;
-   symbols;
-   physical appearance;
-   reputation;
-   a nickname invented locally;
-   the name of a famous leader;
-   an incorrect identification.

Examples:

-   "the Orlocks";
-   "those green-armoured bastards";
-   "the prospectors";
-   "the women from the chem levels";
-   "the bald ones";
-   "Korda's people";
-   "the new toll gang".

Knowledge can improve over time. A mysterious gang in one entry may be
identified by name several entries later.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Preserve Secrets

Never expose hidden campaign information simply because the author knows
it.

Especially protect:

-   Genestealer infection;
-   Chaos allegiance;
-   secret criminal alliances;
-   hidden motives;
-   disguised identities;
-   psychic causes;
-   covert sabotage;
-   factional conspiracies;
-   information known only to gang leadership.

Represent evidence instead.

For example, inhabitants might report:

-   strangely similar families;
-   workers who suddenly behave differently;
-   unusual religious graffiti;
-   people disappearing after meetings;
-   impossible wounds;
-   voices heard through ventilation shafts;
-   unfamiliar contraband;
-   authorities asking unusually specific questions.

The chronicle should allow the reader to infer more than the inhabitants
understand.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Rumour Is a Tool, Not an Excuse

Rumours are useful when they add uncertainty, character, or
foreshadowing.

Good rumours are:

-   plausible from available evidence;
-   culturally appropriate;
-   specific;
-   potentially contradictory;
-   useful later.

Avoid generic constructions such as:

> Some say something darker is happening.

Prefer:

> The sump-workers insist the bodies had already been stripped of their
> teeth before the shooting stopped. The corpse collectors deny it.

Rumours may later prove:

-   correct;
-   partly correct;
-   completely wrong;
-   impossible to verify.

Do not automatically resolve them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Sources and Voices

Entries may be presented through different local forms when appropriate:

-   oral testimony;
-   settlement gossip;
-   market notices;
-   shrine inscriptions;
-   graffiti;
-   worker complaints;
-   tavern stories;
-   Guild warnings;
-   Enforcer notices;
-   vox announcements;
-   casualty lists;
-   missing-person notices;
-   trader correspondence;
-   local songs or sayings;
-   scavenger claims;
-   overheard conversations;
-   anonymous warnings.

Different sources should carry different biases.

An official notice may deny a disaster everyone witnessed. A bartender
may exaggerate it. A child may remember only the sound. A trader may
care exclusively about the closed road.

Necromundan society is fragmented, feudal, violent, and frequently
poorly informed; local accounts should reflect that rather than
producing a single clean historical consensus.

## 12. Official Accounts Are Propaganda

When using Imperial, Guild, House, or Enforcer documentation, assume the
text serves institutional interests.

Official sources may:

-   minimise casualties;
-   redefine a defeat as restoration of order;
-   blame victims;
-   conceal shortages;
-   praise productivity during catastrophe;
-   threaten inhabitants while pretending to reassure them;
-   classify obvious gang warfare as an industrial incident;
-   omit politically dangerous details.

This matches Necromunda's established use of public vox material, where
deadly working conditions, shortages, disease, gang violence, and
infrastructural failures are routinely reframed as matters of duty and
productivity. fileciteturn0file2L159-L180

Do not make every entry official. The point is the inhabitants' world,
not merely the bureaucracy documenting them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Violence Should Feel Normal Until It Isn't

Necromundans live with violence.

A few gunshots, a corpse in an alley, gang intimidation, or a protection
racket may not be extraordinary enough to dominate local memory.

Show the threshold between:

**ordinary violence**

and

**the event people will still talk about ten cycles later.**

A minor firefight may matter because it closes the only water pump.

A dozen dead gangers may matter less than one stray round destroying the
settlement's generator.

Measure importance by **local consequence**, not body count.

Gang warfare is structurally embedded in Necromundan society and
commonly functions as proxy conflict between larger powers.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. Do Not Romanticise the Inhabitants

Locals are not automatically innocent, wise, or united.

They may:

-   exploit refugees;
-   loot corpses;
-   collaborate with whoever is winning;
-   inform on neighbours;
-   profit from shortages;
-   celebrate a hated ganger's death;
-   invent accusations;
-   hide criminals;
-   sell information to both sides;
-   turn a battlefield into a market before the blood dries.

Necromunda should feel like a functioning society adapted to monstrous
conditions, not a modern civilian population temporarily exposed to
them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 15. Keep Necromundan Material Culture Present

Ground entries in physical life.

Use concrete details such as:

-   lumen strips;
-   respirators;
-   recycled air;
-   sump water;
-   corpse-starch;
-   gantries;
-   habs;
-   manufactoria;
-   shrines;
-   bulkheads;
-   ducts;
-   cable runs;
-   promethium;
-   scrap;
-   chem stills;
-   market stalls;
-   Guild caravans;
-   toll stations;
-   servo equipment;
-   abandoned machinery;
-   old tunnels;
-   ash;
-   industrial waste.

The setting should be experienced through what inhabitants touch,
breathe, eat, repair, trade, and fear. Underhive life is explicitly
built among ancient domes, abandoned industrial structures, failing
infrastructure, pollution, and continual gang conflict.

## 16. Territory Type Must Shape the Story

Events should interact with what the territory actually is.

A battle in a **Promethium Cache** should create different stories from
one in a **Settlement**, **Old Ruins**, **Mine Workings**, or **Corpse
Farm**.

Ask:

1.  What do people normally do here?
2.  Who normally lives or works here?
3.  Why does the territory matter?
4.  What can be damaged, stolen, interrupted, contaminated, or
    exploited?
5.  What would inhabitants fear losing?
6.  What would a new controlling gang actually want from the place?
7.  What local habit, profession, economy, or superstition could emerge
    from repeated events?

The territory boon is mechanical shorthand. Convert it into lived
reality.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 17. Translate Mechanics Into Fiction

Never write rules terminology directly into an inhabitant-facing
chronicle unless it also exists naturally in the fiction.

Translate:

-   **Territory captured** → new toll collectors, banners, patrols,
    disappearances;
-   **fighter killed** → body recovered, funeral, corpse missing, local
    landmark;
-   **fighter in recovery** → wounded ganger seen carried away, local
    claim that someone died;
-   **credits gained** → seized cargo, tribute, salvage, protection
    payments;
-   **reputation gained** → locals begin recognising a name or symbol;
-   **rare equipment acquired** → rumours about a new weapon;
-   **alliance formed** → unfamiliar armed outsiders appear beside the
    gang;
-   **scenario objective destroyed** → describe the actual
    infrastructure or object lost;
-   **gang bottled** → locals see one side break and flee;
-   **territory boon** → describe the economic or practical benefit
    inhabitants experience.

Do not mention XP, Reputation points, credit rolls, dice, campaign
phases, scenario rules, or fighter-card states.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 18. Named Fighters Become Local Characters Through Exposure

Do not automatically mention every fighter who participated.

A ganger earns a place in territorial memory by doing something
inhabitants could remember:

-   killing someone publicly;
-   surviving something impossible;
-   repeatedly appearing in the same territory;
-   protecting or terrorising locals;
-   destroying an important structure;
-   being captured;
-   dying spectacularly;
-   making a speech;
-   becoming associated with a distinctive weapon, vehicle, mutation, or
    habit.

Repeated exposure should build local reputations.

A fighter may acquire a nickname in one territory that is unknown
elsewhere.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 19. Death Should Produce Memory

When a named fighter dies in or near a territory, consider leaving a
trace.

Possible traces:

-   shrine;
-   graffiti;
-   warning;
-   nickname for a location;
-   trophy;
-   local joke;
-   ghost story;
-   drinking toast;
-   scavenged equipment;
-   disputed grave;
-   corpse never recovered;
-   false sightings afterward.

Do not force this for every death. Use it when the death was visible,
consequential, or narratively distinctive.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 20. Contradictions Are Allowed

Two entries may disagree.

Two witnesses may disagree inside the same entry.

This is desirable when caused by:

-   limited visibility;
-   propaganda;
-   fear;
-   faction loyalty;
-   intoxication;
-   distance;
-   superstition;
-   deliberate misinformation.

Do not use contradiction to obscure basic campaign facts that would be
universally obvious.

If a territory changed hands, the chronicle may dispute **why**,
**how**, or **at what cost**, but should not casually imply it never
happened unless that itself is deliberate propaganda.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 21. Avoid Omniscient Foreshadowing

Do not write:

> None of them knew that this would be the beginning of the cult's
> uprising.

That voice belongs to an external narrator.

Prefer evidence:

> Three cycles later, someone painted the same four-handed saint above
> the sealed pump station.

Let later events create meaning retroactively.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 22. Avoid Purple Grimdark

Necromunda is already extreme. Do not make every sentence scream about
misery, blood, rust, darkness, damnation, and despair.

Contrast creates credibility.

Include:

-   petty complaints;
-   jokes;
-   commerce;
-   routine;
-   opportunism;
-   bureaucratic absurdity;
-   people adapting;
-   small comforts;
-   local pride.

A noodle stall reopening after a gunfight can make the setting feel more
real than another paragraph about endless suffering.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 23. Tone

Target tone:

-   grounded;
-   specific;
-   laconic when possible;
-   darkly funny when appropriate;
-   materially focused;
-   unreliable without becoming incomprehensible;
-   recognisably Necromundan;
-   interested in consequences rather than spectacle.

Avoid:

-   heroic omniscient narration;
-   generic grimdark poetry;
-   modern journalistic neutrality;
-   tabletop terminology;
-   exposition dumps;
-   explaining mysteries that inhabitants cannot know;
-   making every gang sound legendary;
-   making every event historically important.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 24. Recommended Entry Structure

Use this as the default structure, not a mandatory template.

### `[Event Title]`

**Local date / campaign marker:** if available\
**Territory:** territory name\
**Locally associated factions:** only those plausibly identified

**Event**

Describe what inhabitants experienced.

**Aftermath**

Describe the immediate material or social consequence.

**What people say**

Optional. Include one or more rumours, interpretations, quotes, notices,
or conflicting explanations.

**Persistent change**

Optional. Record anything that should influence later territory entries.

For very small events, collapse all of this into one paragraph.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 25. Event Titles

Titles should sound like names inhabitants might actually give events.

Good:

-   The Night the Pumps Stopped
-   Three Bodies at Gate Seven
-   The Sinter Market Shootout
-   Red Week
-   The Second Burning of Coil Street
-   The Toll War
-   The Quiet Shift
-   When the Lights Went Green
-   The Missing Corpse Collector

Avoid mechanical titles unless they naturally became local terminology:

-   Battle 7
-   Territory Takeover
-   Game vs. Van Saar
-   Scenario: Border Dispute

A minor incident may not need a formal title at all.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 26. Continuity Data to Track Internally

For each territory, maintain an internal continuity record separate from
inhabitant-facing prose.

Track:

-   current mechanical owner;
-   previous owners;
-   known local names for gangs;
-   important inhabitants or witnesses;
-   named fighters remembered locally;
-   damaged infrastructure;
-   repaired infrastructure;
-   shortages;
-   new businesses;
-   shrines and landmarks;
-   graffiti and symbols;
-   unresolved rumours;
-   local superstitions;
-   known routes and closures;
-   recurring hazards;
-   displaced population;
-   local attitudes toward each gang;
-   secrets the inhabitants have partially observed;
-   facts inhabitants definitely know;
-   facts inhabitants definitely do not know.

This prevents later entries from accidentally becoming omniscient or
contradicting established local knowledge.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 27. Local Knowledge Has Geography

Information should spread imperfectly.

An event in one territory may become:

-   common knowledge in adjacent territories;
-   distorted gossip further away;
-   completely unknown elsewhere;
-   known only through House propaganda;
-   associated with the wrong gang by distant inhabitants.

Do not assume every territory shares the same information state.

This matters especially for:

-   secretive gangs;
-   outlaw status;
-   cult activity;
-   deaths;
-   alliances;
-   spectacular weapons;
-   major changes in territorial control.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 28. Campaign State Is Canon; Prose Is Interpretation

When mechanical campaign data and an older narrative entry conflict, the
mechanical campaign state wins regarding:

-   ownership;
-   fighter status;
-   deaths;
-   injuries;
-   gang composition;
-   alliances;
-   campaign results;
-   other rules-defined state.

Do not silently rewrite the local account into omniscient truth.

Instead distinguish:

**what objectively happened** from **what the inhabitants recorded or
believed**.

An incorrect rumour can remain canonically incorrect.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 29. Minimum Transformation Rule

Never convert campaign data into prose by merely replacing game
terminology with setting terminology.

Bad transformation:

> The Iron Serpents defeated the Ash Dogs and captured the Promethium
> Cache.

Better transformation:

> The shooting around Tank Farm Nine lasted less than an hour. By second
> shift, the Ash Dogs were gone and the Serpents had men on every fuel
> gantry. Promethium merchants now pay twice: once to the Guild and once
> to whoever is holding the Serpents' collection tin.

The second version creates territory history.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 30. Final Test

Before accepting an entry, check:

1.  **Could an inhabitant plausibly know everything stated here?**
2.  **Does the territory matter to the event, or could this text happen
    anywhere?**
3.  **What changed for the people who live here?**
4.  **Did mechanics become fiction rather than remain mechanics?**
5.  **Were campaign secrets protected?**
6.  **Does the entry remember previous events where relevant?**
7.  **Is anything presented as fact that should actually be rumour?**
8.  **Is the event being exaggerated beyond its campaign importance?**
9.  **Did the gangs dominate the prose so completely that the
    inhabitants disappeared?**
10. **Has this entry added at least one usable piece of persistent
    territorial history?**

If the answer to the final question is no, the event probably does not
need a territory chronicle entry.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Governing Rule

> **Record not what the players know happened, but what the people who
> have to live there believe happened to their home.**
